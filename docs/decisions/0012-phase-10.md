## 3. Finding P10-FA — third-party client network loads

> Provenance: P10-R0 authorization appendix. Verbatim-as-supplied: YES.
> Status: ratified.
> Marking (P10-R1): verbatim under the P10-N1 state-1 definition — the
> single `\<link\>` escape is Markdown-only escaping of the source's
> literal `<link>`.

Basis.

Remote asset loading from a client that renders private correspondence is
a disclosure channel, not UI residue.

A remote image URL causes the reader's browser to contact a third-party
host. That host learns the reader's IP address and the moment of reading.
Where the URL is supplied by a sender, the sender learns when a specific
recipient opened a specific letter — recipient telemetry obtained without
any act by the recipient and without their knowledge.

This is a bounded-disclosure violation delivered through styling. It
survived the Phase 0.5 constitutional strip because it did not look like
a mechanic.

Ruling.

No third-party network load of any kind is permitted from the client in
this slice.

Specifically prohibited:

- remote font loading, including Google Fonts @import and \<link\>;
- remote stylesheet or script loading from any origin;
- remote image rendering, including seed/sample imagery;
- any surface accepting a user-supplied remote URL for rendering;
- any replacement network dependency introduced to restore visual
  fidelity.

All fonts and assets are local.

`photo` remains declared and unimplemented under 0007. This finding does
not implement it, reserve for it, or authorize a local-upload substitute.

Prospective rule.

Any future remote fetch originating from a surface that renders private
correspondence returns to chamber before implementation. This holds
regardless of the load's apparent innocuousness — analytics, fonts,
avatars, embeds, previews, and error reporting are all within scope.

Enforcement split across 10.A.

P10-A3 enforces the manifest, lockfile, and CSS/font portion.

P10-A4 enforces the remainder, because the surviving vectors (the seed
image URL, the user-supplied image URL path) are owned by files
physically deleted in that commit.

Per P10-C1 and P10-D1, P10-A3 does not reach into P10-A4-owned files to
satisfy this finding. The complete B4 sweep proving P10-FA is an exit
condition of P10-A4.

### P10-J6 — CITATION VERSUS TRANSMISSION-INTERNAL NUMBERING

> Provenance: P10-R20 authorization, drafted against the source gate examined
> in the 8c packet. Status: ratified. Marking (P10-N1): the operative clauses
> are the wording the chamber authorized without addition; this stanza and the
> heading are state 4 builder documentary prose. P10-J5's own text is
> unamended; P10-J6 sits beside it and supplies the outcome J5 lacks.

An identifier appearing in a chamber transmission is a **citation** only when
it resolves to committed text in this ledger or another committed decision
record. Resolution is the test.

An identifier that carries its own operative text and resolves to nothing is
**transmission-internal numbering**. It is not a citation. The law it carries
is **new law requiring filing**, whatever its numbering suggests.

An identifier **silent about whether it cites or rules** is classified by
committed resolution first. It is never classified by tone, by completeness of
its operative language, by resemblance to an existing series, by the use of a
section symbol, or by the confidence with which it is transmitted. An
identifier presented as settled law that resolves to nothing is
transmission-internal numbering presented as citation, and is treated as the
former.

Operative law transmitted **without any identifier** is likewise unfiled law.
Absence of a label does not exempt it from filing; a J5 comparison that finds a
gate's operative content unrepresented in the ledger reports it, identifier or
not.

A P10-J5 comparison reports transmission-carried operative law that does not
resolve in the ledger as **UNFILED**. It is never reported as PRESENT.

**UNFILED is not automatically a missing-law STOP.** Where the transmission
itself contains complete operative text and expressly issues that text as new
law, the builder proceeds against the operative text, records the law as
unfiled, and carries a **required filing act** into the completion report. The
gate is not blocked by the ledger's silence about law the gate itself supplies.

Where an identifier resolves to nothing **and carries no operative text**, it
is reported UNFILED and is a **STOP**. The transmitting chamber supplies the
operative text or withdraws the citation. The builder does not supply a nearest
match, does not infer the missing provision, and does not proceed on the
assumption that the identifier meant something recoverable.

The rule covers both failure directions: **operative text without a committed
filing**, and **a purported filing without operative text**.

No future gate may treat transmission-internal numbering as a committed
citation merely because it uses a section symbol, resembles an existing series,
or carries complete operative language.

*Provenance note: recorded after a five-member §13.x series governed an
accepted sub-step while existing nowhere in the committed record. Of the three
members examined, one expressly declared itself a ruling and two were presented
as standing law; none resolved. The classification test keys on resolution
rather than presentation because presentation was the unreliable signal in
every instance.*

### §13.6 — CORRESPONDENCES FIRST AUTHORITATIVE SLICE — RULED

**1. ONTOLOGY**

Correspondence is not a first-class object.

It has:

- no independent identifier;
- no persistence apart from Placement;
- no lifecycle of its own;
- no database table;
- no mutation;
- no domain event;
- no authorization rule beyond Placement readability.

Correspondences is an actor-relative, read-only projection over committed
Placement records.

No new ontology term is added to `domain/`.

**2. UNIT OF DERIVATION**

The first authoritative slice is pairwise.

For the active acting Self, one Correspondence is derived per visible
counterpart Self.

The derivation key is the counterpart Self id relative to the active acting
Self.

A Correspondence is therefore:

  active acting Self ↔ one counterpart Self

It is not:

- a Self-to-recipient-set object;

### §13.6-A — CORRESPONDENCES PRESENTATION — RULED

**1. LABELSELVES SCOPE**

Correspondences may use `labelSelves`.

P10-T3 commits the collision rule to the switcher. The rule proposed to bar the
Prism from `labelSelves` was P10-T6, which is UNFILED. Committed law authorizes
`labelSelves` for one surface and is silent on others. This ruling supplies that
silence for Correspondences and for no other surface.

The reasoning that kept the Prism off `labelSelves` does not transfer. The Prism
presents one Self as identity, and routing identity through a presentation shape
would carry a storage coordinate into an identity claim. Correspondences
presents several counterparts side by side, where a collision is visible and is
the presentation problem `labelSelves` was built to solve.

Collision determination is performed over the counterpart set actually presented
on the Correspondences surface, not over the authenticated account's entire
`SelfSummary[]`.

A counterpart is distinguished only where another displayed counterpart shares
its authoritative name. A same-named Self that is not a member of the presented
counterpart set contributes nothing to the label.

Groups are identified to the human by the `labelSelves` label of the counterpart
Self.

**2. WHAT IS NOT SHOWN**

No internal Self id is displayed.

No per-group settled-Placement count is shown. R6's count prohibitions are
Prism-scoped and are not extended here; the exclusion is on its own reasoning —
a count is a quantity signal the first slice does not need.

No individual Placement content appears in the first authoritative slice.
Group-level identification only.

**3. SELECTION**

Groups are presented and are not selectable in P10-S12.

§13.6 already forbids automatic selection. This ruling adds that manual
selection is also absent from the first slice: selection is a later slice with
its own ruling.

**4. NAME-SOURCE DEPENDENCY, RECORDED**

Every counterpart resolves to a name in `SelfSummary[]` because R1 confines
Phase 10 to one account and draws recipients exclusively from
`GET /auth/selves`.

R1 also forbids the substitute — no foreign-Self name-resolution surface, no
directory. A cross-account counterpart would be an id with no name obtainable by
permitted means.

The naming mechanism is an artifact of the account boundary and expires with it.
This is recorded as a dependency, not a design choice that survives scope growth.

**5. SINGLE-SENDER DEPENDENCY, RECORDED**

The pairwise derivation takes counterpart = `senderSelfId` for received
Placements. That is sufficient only because the committed Placement model has
exactly one sender per Placement.

The derivation does not generalize to any future model permitting multiple
senders. The dependency is recorded rather than inferred.

### §13.6-B — CORRESPONDENCES FAILURE SEMANTICS — RULED

**1. ALL-OR-NONE**

Derivation is all-or-none.

Any failure in the placements read, or in any recipients read, yields no
projection. The surface reports unavailability.

Explicitly-incomplete derivation is declined: it would require the surface to
name what is missing, and naming an absent Placement to its author is content
this slice has no law for.

**2. UNAVAILABILITY IS NOT EMPTINESS**

A failed read is presented distinctly from a valid empty set.

A valid empty set is 2xx with an empty array on the placements read, and yields
zero groups with no placeholder, per §13.6.

Failure never renders as zero groups.

**3. THE EMPTY-RECIPIENTS CASE**

A 2xx empty recipient array for an authored settled Placement is not a valid
projection outcome.

It contradicts a committed Placement invariant: a departure requires at least
one recipient, enforced at PT409, with Key requiring exactly one.

The chamber does not treat it as an empty recipient set. It is classified as an
invalid authoritative response and enters the failure branch rather than the
successful derivation branch.

**4. MALFORMED, 401, 403**

A non-array body at either layer is invalid and enters the failure branch.

401 routes to the existing session-expired transition.

403 routes to the existing forbidden transition with its single re-verification.

Neither is absorbed into unavailability.

**5. PENDING**

Pending is a first-class surface state.

While the read is outstanding the surface presents neither groups nor
unavailability.

Pending is distinguishable from both successful projection and unavailability in
both state and presentation.

A two-state encoding that collapses pending into either successful projection or
unavailability does not satisfy this ruling.

**6. RE-ATTEMPT AND RETURN**

All-or-none makes recovery load-bearing. Continue triggers the read and is
reachable only from the Prism floor, so a failed read with no path back
reproduces the permanence the P10-S11 corrective was issued to remove.

Correspondences returns to the Prism floor by an in-memory surface-state
transition.

That return is available from both the unavailable state and the presented
state.

The return introduces no router, URL, history, persistence, fetch, selection,
active-Self change, or additional behavior. It is the inverse of Continue's
transition and nothing more.

A subsequent Continue activation re-attempts the read.

That is the ruled re-attempt path: one attempt per deliberate human act, no
polling, interval, focus refetch, background refresh, or automatic retry.

### P10-S12.1 — ONE FORBIDDEN DISPOSITION FOR EVERY SELF-SCOPED READ

A 403 from a Self-scoped client request has one constitutional meaning: the
client may no longer continue acting as the current Self on the authority
presently held. That meaning does not vary by surface.

The authored-Artifact count read, the Correspondences placements read, any
authored-placement recipients read, and any later Self-scoped read introduced in
Phase 10 may not define independent 403 dispositions.

A surface may determine what it renders after the transition. It may not
determine whether the active Self is forgotten, whether persisted selection is
retained, or whether re-verification occurs.

Every Self-scoped 403 routes through the existing forbidden transition,
`onForbidden`, which remains authoritative for discarding the in-memory active
Self, discarding the persisted per-tab active-Self id, performing exactly one
re-verification against `/auth/selves`, and returning to Self selection where the
active Self cannot be restored lawfully. That behavior is not duplicated inline,
no surface-specific approximation is created, and the persisted id is not
retained after a Self-scoped 403.

A 401 routes through the existing session-expired transition, `onSessionExpired`.
A 401 is not treated as unavailability, an unknown count, an empty
Correspondences result, a 403, or an ordinary recoverable data failure. The 401
and 403 transitions remain distinct even where both ultimately remove the current
surface.

Non-auth failure semantics are unchanged and remain local to each read: a
non-auth unavailable count releases the in-memory active Self to selection while
retaining the persisted id; Correspondences non-auth failure remains all-or-none
unavailability, clears neither the active Self nor the persisted id, and remains
re-attemptable through Continue. Authorization failure and data unavailability
are separate state transitions.

*Ledger note (state 4) — the audit that produced this ruling, and the four
divergences it established.* The committed client had exactly three Self-scoped
reads: `GET /artifacts`, `GET /placements`, and `GET /placements/:id/recipients`.
The audit established four divergent authorization paths: (1) `GET /artifacts`
on 403; (2) `GET /artifacts` on 401; (3) `GET /placements` on 403; (4)
`GET /placements/:id/recipients` on 403. The count read classified neither 401
nor 403 — every non-2xx response collapsed into an unknown count. The two
Correspondences reads classified 403 correctly but handled it inline, without
`onForbidden`, without discarding the persisted id, and without the single
re-verification. **Three request paths are governed; the fourth divergence is
the count read's 401.** P10-S11's wiring limitation 8 — that a 403 from the count
request reaches `onForbidden` — was not merely unproven but false, and is
recorded as a corrected behavioral defect. **The count-read 401 gap was named by
nothing and is recorded as an independently discovered defect, distinct from that
reclassified limitation.*

### P10-S12.2 — COMPLETION AND CONTAINMENT OF THE FORBIDDEN TRANSITION

Regardless of whether the single re-verification returns a response or rejects:
the in-memory active Self is cleared; the persisted per-tab id remains discarded;
the surface leaves Correspondences pending; no rejected promise escapes uncaught;
and the client does not continue asserting the forbidden Self. These requirements
complete R3's forbidden transition and are mandatory, not a product preference.

A mechanism that performs only the required mutations in `finally` but allows the
rejection to escape does not satisfy this ruling. A mechanism that catches the
rejection but does not guarantee the required mutations on every path also does
not satisfy it. Both completion of the state mutations and containment of the
rejected promise are required.

**Ordering is not ruled.** R3 lists discard before re-verification but does not
expressly compel that order; it does not use "then" or "before" between those two
requirements, and the unconditionality of the present discard-before-reverify
implementation has no committed textual source. This does not reorder
`onForbidden` and does not make the existing ordering constitutional law.

**Minimal module shape.** The pure settlement module does not preserve or expose
a distinction between a completed non-auth response and a rejecting attempt after
settlement. Both branches yield the same settled transition state. The module
returns no completed-versus-rejected discriminator, no reason code, no error
taxonomy, no terminal-surface instruction, and no authentication classification.
The distinction remains relevant to evidence and provenance, not to the
settled-state return type, because the separate gate disposition was withdrawn.
A future ruling may reopen that return shape only if it assigns the
completed-response branch a distinct terminal behavior; implementation may not
reopen it independently.

**Extraction necessity.** The pure module was authorized because the required
behavioral properties cannot be proven by source-text inspection, the test
environment has no installed DOM implementation, and placing the transition in
the application shell would contradict the committed pure-state-module boundary.
It is not extraction for convenience.

### §1 · AUTHORITATIVE CLIENT ARCHITECTURE

**1.1 Module law.** Three module kinds, none absorbing another's
responsibility:

- **state modules** — synchronous, transport-free, callback-free; own state
  and pure transitions;
- **mutation modules** — own one Self-scoped HTTP boundary and its status
  contract;
- **orchestration modules** — own asynchronous sequencing and the injected
  authorization dispositions.

*Normalized from P10-S13 §3 and applied unchanged through P10-S19.*

**1.2 One authoritative value per concern.** The client holds exactly one

⟨documentary⟩ *This is a normalized statement appearing verbatim in no single

- `not-started` — the original state, by identity, and **no request**; or
- `started` — the **already-applied pending state** and exactly one settlement

The pending state therefore exists in the caller's world before asynchronous

     · cancelling-pending · cancellation-failed · cancelled
     · settling-pending   · settlement-failed   · settled

**2.3 Terminality is enforced at the transition boundary.** `cancelled` and
`settled` are terminal. Terminality is established by **refusal of the act**,
not by omission of a control: the production boundary returns `not-started`
with the state returned by identity and issues no request. Control omission is
not an adequate guard. *P10-S18 §§1, 5, ruled for cancellation; applied to
settlement at P10-S19.*

**2.4 The server-idempotence asymmetry, recorded not smoothed.**
`settle_placement` returns early on an already-settled row and would answer
**204** to a repeat. Client terminality for `settled` is therefore **the
client's own rule**, not a server rejection. *P10-S19.*

**2.5 Cancellation is not a return to draft.** The committed transition is
`departing → cancelled`. Recipients stay frozen, the same Placement can never
depart again, and retrying means a new draft and a new Placement. *P10-S18
opening correction, proven against live source.*

**2.6 Joint settlement results.** Every lifecycle mutation settles through
**one** value carrying every effect together — `{ lifecycle, retainedDraft }` —
never through independent setters. Simultaneity is the content of the
exclusivity rule: a Placement must never be representable as retained draft and
departing, or cancelled and departing, at the same instant. *P10-S17
(`DepartureSettlement`), normalized by P10-S18 §2 and carried unchanged into
P10-S19.*

**2.7 Recipients.** Add and removal are deliberate acts, one pending mutation
at a time across both. Success is 204-only, with no optimistic application;
removal exists only for locally known recipients. The recipient set is **frozen
at departure** by the committed server trigger, and the client's representation
freezes at the same moment. **Corrections after that are new acts, never
mutation of old acts.** *P10-S15, P10-S16, P10-S17.*

**2.8 Settlement is client-initiated.** No automatic settlement path exists —
no timer, interval, or loop reaches the settlement boundary — and the server
alone enforces the snapshotted interval. Settle remains available while
departing. *P10-S19.*

### §7 · FORWARD AUTHORITY

The rulings below bind the opening and execution of Segment 10.E. They do not
amend, reinterpret, or retroactively govern any accepted Segment 10.D ruling,
gate, completion report, or acceptance. They do govern how the carried bindings
recorded by those sub-steps are classified, evidenced, and discharged, because
those bindings remain open and their evidence has not yet been produced.
**Classifying an open binding is not a reinterpretation of the accepted record
that created it.**

**7.1 · Evidence classes.** Every carried binding belongs to **exactly one** of
three dispositions: **Class A**, **Class B**, or **reported ambiguity under
§5A**.

**Class A — discharged by evidence available within a mounted client
environment.** Typical: App supplies an injected dependency; a mounted control
invokes it; App applies a returned production transition; mounted presentation
reflects an already-observed client state. **Compilation, source inspection,
and pure-transition tests do not close Class A. Mounted observation is
required.**

**Class B — requires observation across a real production boundary.** Typical:
a real server response corresponding to a production transition; authoritative
interval behavior; row-lock race resolution; outbox processing; any behavior
whose evidence depends on a running server rather than an injected boundary.
**Neither source inspection nor injected transports discharge Class B.**

**7.2 · Independent-evidence rule.** A binding is discharged only by evidence
**independent of the claim**. A mounted client using an injected recording
transport proves the client's behavior *after receiving a response*; it does
not prove the client receives that response *from a real server*. Bindings
requiring a real production boundary close only against a real server response.
**Every mounted flow reports the transport it used.**

**7.3 · Classification precedes inventory.** Every carried binding is
classified **first**. Only after classification is complete may implementation
inventory, scope, or segmentation be determined.

**7.4 · §5A — classification ambiguity.** A binding whose wording honestly
spans more than one evidence class is **not split, narrowed, or reinterpreted
by the builder**. The builder reports: the exact binding; the competing honest
readings; the evidence boundary each reading would require; and why no single
classification preserves the wording.

The chamber then rules one of four dispositions: **narrow the binding to one
class**; **split it into two bindings**; **retain it unchanged and require both
evidence boundaries before it closes**; or **another expressly stated
constitutional disposition**. **The builder does not choose among those
outcomes.**

A ruled split **increases the register by one**. That increment records
**clarification of an existing claim** and is never reported as a newly
discovered limitation, a newly discovered behavioral defect, or additional
engineering scope. The completion report states that the increase arose from
constitutional clarification rather than implementation.

**The standing register increases for exactly three reasons:**

1. a genuinely new carried limitation is discovered;
2. a previously counted overlap is **dissolved on evidence**;
3. a constitutional clarification **splits one claim into two** independently
   verifiable claims.

**Every increment states which of the three it is. An increment that cannot
name one is a finding, not an increment.**

**7.5 · Classification report.** Before any Segment 10.E implementation begins,
a **complete classification report** is returned, stating for every carried
binding: its wording; its evidence class; the evidence required; whether it is
immediately dischargeable; and any §5A ambiguity. **No implementation inventory
is prepared before this report.**

**7.6 · Segment shape.** The completed classification determines Segment 10.E's
scope. If the Class B count is small enough to execute alongside Class A
without obscuring constitutional accounting, 10.E remains one segment;
otherwise the chamber determines whether Class B opens as a subsequent segment.
**The builder reports. The chamber rules.**

### 54.5 · Mechanism-only rulings — governing doctrine

**P10-CT — the creation transition.**

> A production transition may be traversed as mechanism without opening,
> proving, partially proving, or closing any proposition governing that
> transition.
>
> Reachability and constitutional evidence are distinct objects.

A mechanism-only traversal establishes **exactly one proposition: that the later
constitutional object became reachable through the production implementation.**
It establishes neither correctness, completeness, exclusivity, uniqueness,
wiring, state application, persistence, restoration, authorization, settlement,
callback origin, dependency ownership, nor production validity.

> successful traversal ↛ correct implementation
> successful traversal ↛ shared wiring
> successful traversal ↛ correct state transition
> successful traversal ↛ correct callback origin
> successful traversal ↛ correct authorization
> successful traversal ↛ proof of the transition
> successful traversal ↛ closure of any creation-transition binding

**P10-RA — the recipient-add path.**

> A recipient-add transition may be traversed as mechanism without opening,
> proving, partially proving, or closing any proposition governing recipient
> addition.
>
> The presence of a recipient in a later production state establishes
> reachability only. It does not establish the constitutional correctness of how
> that recipient arrived there.

**Both remain mechanism only.** A traversal appears only as `ROUTE:` or
*mechanism reached*, and never under constitutional object, observation,
evidence, evidence classification, proposed disposition, or closure. **A route
failure prevents a conclusion about the later target binding and classifies no
binding governing the route. A successful later observation supplies no evidence
that the route was correct.**
